FX coverage · Credit operations

Enforce FX coverage at drawdown.

Investors fund credit originators in dollars; those originators lend to businesses that repay in local currency. If that currency falls, the dollar value of the loan book falls with it.

Traditionally, the originator promises to maintain FX cover as a condition of the loan. But that covenant is checked on a reporting cycle, so a hedge can lapse while new capital is still drawn.

Signa Covenant checks authenticated FX coverage at each drawdown. If cover is insufficient, capital does not move.

02 / The mechanism

Signa Covenant checks coverage first.

The vault reads a signed statement of FX cover at drawdown. If the cover is current and large enough, it releases capital; if not, it stops the draw.

  1. 01

    The investor sets the terms first

    The investor sets how much of the loan the hedge must cover, how old the evidence may be, and how much cash the vault keeps back. Nobody can change those terms afterwards, so every later decision meets the same standard.

    Facility Registry
  2. 02

    Two parties sign what they can see

    The servicer signs a statement of what the borrower owes. The hedge provider signs a statement of what the hedge covers. Each signature must match a key the investor approved beforehand, so neither party can restate the other's figure. Mock issuers in this demonstration.

    Credential Registry
  3. 03

    A genuine signature is not enough

    A statement can be genuine and still count for nothing: too old, the wrong currencies, or a hedge that ends before the loans repay. What survives is cut by five per cent, in case the hedge settles short, then capped at the size of the loan.

    Coverage Engine
  4. 04

    The vault runs the test as the money moves

    A withdrawal request makes the vault re-read both statements and measure them against the rule, inside the same transaction. Enough cover and the dollars leave; short cover and the vault refuses, recording why.
