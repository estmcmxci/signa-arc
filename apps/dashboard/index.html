FX coverage · Credit operations

A covenant that governs the money, not the conversation.

A covenant is a condition a borrower must keep true. Lenders test covenants on a reporting calendar; borrowers draw cash whenever they like. This vault tests the cover itself, inside the transaction that pays out, and refuses the drawdown when cover falls short.

01 / The problem

Capital moves on demand.
Reconciliation runs on a calendar.

A hedge settles short on Tuesday. The servicer, the firm that administers the loans, checks the records on Friday. In between, the vault pays out against a figure that no longer holds, and the lender carries risk it never priced.

Timing creates the risk, not bad records.

02 / The mechanism

One rule, from evidence to release.

A lender advances dollars against loans repaid in euros. If the euro weakens first, the lender bears the shortfall, unless someone has promised to sell euros at a fixed price. That promise is the hedge. It sits at a bank, so the vault demands a signed statement of it.

  1. 01

    The lender sets the terms first

    The lender sets how much of the loan the hedge must cover, how old the evidence may be, and how much cash the vault keeps back. Nobody can change those terms afterwards, so every later decision meets the same standard.

    Facility Registry
  2. 02

    Two parties sign what they can see

    The servicer signs a statement of what the borrower owes. The hedge provider signs a statement of what the hedge covers. Each signature must match a key the lender approved beforehand, so neither party can restate the other's figure. Mock issuers in this demonstration.

    Credential Registry
  3. 03

    A genuine signature is not enough

    A statement can be genuine and still count for nothing: too old, the wrong currencies, or a hedge that ends before the loans repay. What survives is cut by five per cent, in case the hedge settles short, then capped at the size of the loan.

    Coverage Engine
  4. 04

    The vault runs the test as the money moves

    A withdrawal request makes the vault re-read both statements and measure them against the rule, inside the same transaction. Enough cover and the dollars leave; short cover and the vault refuses, recording why.

03 / What it enforces

The rule travels with the money.

Coverage checked during the draw

The contract evaluates the evidence in the same transaction that releases funds. A previous green screen is no substitute for that check.

Signed does not mean counted

Age, approval, currencies and end date decide what counts toward cover. When a statement is excluded, the contract says which of those it failed.

Distinct issuers, explicit limits

Exposure and hedge require different approved issuer keys. This enforces key separation, not legal independence between firms.

An exception leaves a record

A waiver is dated and attributable. The recorded admin waiver required both the risk officer and treasury lead to approve.

Settlement and network fees use USDC on Arc Testnet. The application does not price, recommend or execute a derivative.

04 / The recorded proof

The same draw.
Three decisions.

The same withdrawal, requested three times with identical instructions. The vault paid the first, refused the second after a hedge settled short, and paid the third once cover returned. It checked the cover inside each transaction, not on a report afterwards.
